### [1] [Machine learning reconstruction of digit bone Raman spectra enables noninvasive transcutaneous detection of systemic osteoporosis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18983)
*Mohammad Hosseini,Sadia Afrin,Anthony Yosick,Hani Awad,Andrew J. Berger*

Main category: physics.med-ph

TL;DR: Transcutaneous SORS combined with supervised ML can reconstruct exposed-bone Raman spectra from through-tissue measurements in human cadavers, discriminating normal vs osteoporotic bone and correlating with DXA T-scores; preliminary in vivo data support feasibility.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: DXA is gold standard but limited by ionizing radiation, cost and access; a nonionizing, portable optical method for direct biochemical bone assessment would enable broader osteoporosis screening. SORS can probe subsurface tissue but needs computational extraction to isolate bone signal.

Method: Paired Raman measurements were acquired from transcutaneous fingers at multiple spatial offsets (0, 3, 6 mm) and from exposed finger bones in human cadaveric donors across normal, osteopenic, and osteoporotic groups. Supervised ML models were trained to map transcutaneous spectra to exposed-bone spectra. Reconstructed spectra were analyzed for Raman-derived metrics and correlated with distal-radius DXA T-scores. Preliminary in vivo measurements were obtained from two volunteers.

Result: ML-predicted bone spectra preserved key physiologic Raman features and showed statistically significant differences between normal and osteoporotic groups across four metrics (p < 0.05). Predicted spectra correlated with DXA T-scores (r = 0.73, RMSECV = 1.4), approaching exposed-bone performance (r = 0.9, RMSECV = 0.8). Two in vivo volunteers produced bone-related transcutaneous spectral features consistent with cadaveric findings.

Conclusion: The study demonstrates feasibility of nonionizing, transcutaneous Raman assessment of bone health via supervised spectral extraction. It provides a foundation for further clinical validation, larger cohorts, instrumentation optimization, and robustness testing to move toward a practical screening tool.

Abstract: Osteoporosis, a major global epidemic, often goes undetected until a fracture occurs, largely due to poor access to screening using gold standard methods, such as dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA). The ML-predicted spectra further correlated with distal-radius DXA T-scores (r = 0.73, RMSECV = 1.4), approaching the performance achieved using exposed-bone measurements (r = 0.9, RMSECV = 0.8). Finally, preliminary in vivo measurements from two volunteers revealed clear bone-related transcutaneous spectral features consistent with cadaveric data, supporting translational feasibility. Together, these results establish a foundation for nonionizing radiation, transcutaneous Raman assessment of bone health using supervised spectral extraction from accessible measurement sites
