### [1] [Physics-informed neural networks for shock capturing in inviscid flows around an airfoil](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08130)
*Jiahao Song,Wenbo Cao,Weiwei Zhang*

Main category: physics.flu-dyn

TL;DR: Introduce unsteady Euler constraints and a pseudo time-stepping PDE loss to guide PINNs toward steady shock solutions, avoiding smooth local optima and improving shock capture around airfoils; validated on four forward problems.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: PINNs often fail to capture discontinuous shock solutions for steady inviscid flows because the steady Euler residuals form weak constraints and neural networks prefer smooth approximations, causing convergence to smooth local minima.

Method: Reconstruct steady shock-capturing as a temporal evolution problem by adding time-derivative terms (unsteady Euler equations) to the loss so shocks form gradually. Introduce a pseudo time-stepping PDE loss to avoid long real-time integration and propose a simplified Euler formulation to improve accuracy. Train PINNs to evolve under temporal laws toward steady shock-containing solutions.

Result: Applied to four forward problems with varying flow conditions and geometries; the proposed approach reportedly improved shock-capturing accuracy compared to standard PINNs, demonstrating avoidance of smooth local optima and formation of shocks during training.

Conclusion: Strengthening physical constraints via unsteady evolution and pseudo time-stepping reduces the tendency of PINNs to produce overly smooth solutions and enhances their capability to recover steady shocks, though success may depend on pseudo-time design, loss weighting, and numerical stability.

Abstract: Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) have shown remarkable prospects in solving forward and inverse problems involving partial differential equations (PDEs). However, PINNs still face challenges in solving fluid mechanics problems involving shocks, especially in steady inviscid flows around an airfoil, where they may even fail to capture shocks. In this study, we first point out that the reason PINNs fail to capture shocks is that the steady Euler equations used to construct the loss function impose weak constraints, which are difficult to correct the continuous function approximation preference of neural networks, causing gradient descent converges to a smooth local optimum. Based on this insight, we propose to strengthen the physical constraints by reconstructing steady shock capturing as temporal evolution that gradually converges to the steady state solution. The unsteady Euler equations constructed by introducing time derivative terms into the steady equations are used to constrain PINNs. The output of PINNs is no longer required to directly approximate a flow field with shocks by minimizing the residuals of the steady Euler equations. Instead, shocks gradually form under the guidance of the temporal evolution law of the flow field. This additional temporal penalty alleviates the tendency of PINNs to converge to a smooth local optimum. Since obtaining the steady state solution requires solving the unsteady Euler equations over a long time in the time dimension, while the capability of PINNs to solve such problems is poor, we introduce a PDE loss function that embeds the concept of pseudo time-stepping to avoid this issue. In addition, to further improve the shock capturing accuracy, we develop a simplified formulation of the Euler equations. By solving four forward problems involving different flow conditions and geometries, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

### [2] [Trapping-loss transition via a saddle-node bifurcation in thermophoretic particle transport driven by a time-periodic vortex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08223)
*Srikumar Warrier*

Main category: physics.flu-dyn

TL;DR: Outward inertia-induced drift competes with inward thermophoresis in a time-periodic vortex with a localized temperature field. Using the small-Stokes-number limit, the authors derive a cycle-averaged radial ODE whose fixed points correspond to trapped or separatrix states. A dimensionless parameter Π controls the balance; when Π drops below a critical value the stable and unstable fixed points annihilate in a saddle-node bifurcation, causing loss of trapping and critical slowing down. The mechanism is robust to changes in velocity and temperature profiles.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Understand how competing migration mechanisms (particle inertia vs thermophoresis) determine long-time particle organization in time-periodic vortical flows and identify the conditions for trapping vs ejection.

Method: Start from particle equations of motion in the small-Stokes-number limit, perform cycle-averaging over the forcing period to derive a reduced slow radial dynamics model. Analyze fixed points, linear stability, bifurcations (phase portraits, bifurcation diagrams), and perform local asymptotic analysis near the critical point. Validate robustness by repeating with various velocity and temperature profiles.

Result: The reduced model exhibits inward (thermophoretic) and outward (inertia-induced) contributions; their competition yields a pair of fixed points (stable trap and unstable separatrix). As the control parameter Π (ratio of inertia to thermophoresis) is decreased past a critical Π_c, the pair collides and disappears via a saddle-node bifurcation. The dominant eigenvalue vanishes at the transition and the relaxation time diverges (critical slowing down). Numerical tests with other profiles confirm robustness.

Conclusion: Particle trapping in the studied unsteady vortex is governed by a single dimensionless parameter Π; trapping is lost through a saddle-node bifurcation when thermophoresis dominates sufficiently. The phenomenon and its critical signatures are robust across profile choices, though results rely on the small-Stokes-number approximation.

Abstract: The transport of inertial particles in unsteady flows is often governed by the competition between multiple migration mechanisms. We investigate the interplay between inertia-induced drift and thermophoretic migration in a time-periodic vortex containing a localized temperature field. Starting from the small-Stokes-number limit of the particle equations of motion, we derive a cycle-averaged radial migration model describing the slow evolution of suspended particles over timescales much longer than the forcing period. The competition between outward inertia-induced drift and inward thermophoretic migration gives rise to stable and unstable fixed points of the reduced radial dynamics, corresponding respectively to particle trapping states and separatrices bounding trapped trajectories. The existence and location of these states are shown to be governed by the dimensionless control parameter $Π$, which measures the relative strength of inertia-induced transport to the thermophoretic transport. As $Π$ is decreased below a critical value, the stable and unstable fixed points coalesce and disappear, resulting in the loss of particle trapping. Phase portraits, bifurcation diagrams, and local asymptotic analysis demonstrate that trapping is destroyed through a saddle-node bifurcation. The transition is further characterized by the vanishing of the dominant eigenvalue and the associated divergence of the relaxation time, indicative of critical slowing down. Additional calculations employing various other velocity and temperature profiles demonstrate that the trapping-loss mechanism is robust and not specific to a particular profile.

Application to a coupled Oldroyd--B free-surface film reveals genuine folds of the neutral-saddle manifold and a re-entrant CI--AI--CI boundary geometry for the selected saddle family. TL;DR: PreSPA는 구조적 열화(무참조)와 텍스처 잡음(부분참조 μ) 두 지표로 품질을 분해해, 참조 정보는 단일 스칼라 μ만 사용하고 아핀 융합으로 최종 점수를 내는 경량·투명한 Partial-Reference IQA 프레임워크다.

Motivation: 완전참조(FR) 성능은 유지하면서 참조 요구량을 최소화하고, No-Reference(NR)의 범용성에 근접한 정확도를 달성하려는 목적.

Method: 구조 지표: Hermite–Gauss 기반 왜곡 그래디언트 예측과 곡률의 각도 분산을 무참조로 계산. 텍스처 지표: 강엣지 영역의 복소 그래디언트 에너지 차이를 약구조 영역에 누적해 얻은 스칼라 우선 μ로 국지적 노이즈를 추정. μ는 이미지 쌍당 한 번만 계산. 최종 점수는 3개의 해석 가능한 파라미터로 이루어진 아핀 결합.

Result: 6개 벤치마크에서 주요 NR 기법들과 대등하거나 우수한 성능을 보였고, 몇몇 경우 FR 모델과 동등한 정확도를 기록.

Conclusion: 참조 발자국을 정보론적 최소로 줄이면서 해석성·효율성을 확보한 실용적 접근. 그러나 μ의 단일 스칼라 요약이 모든 왜곡 유형을 포착하는지, 엣지 검출·스케일 선택의 민감성 등은 추가 검증이 필요하다.

Abstract: We propose PreSPA (Partial-Reference Structural Prediction Approach), a Partial-Reference Image Quality Assessment framework that decomposes perceptual quality into two complementary indices. A structure-aware index, operating in a No-Reference manner, captures structural degradation through Hermite-Gauss prediction of the distorted gradient field and the angular variance of its curvature. A texture-sensitive index estimates local noise through a scalar prior $μ$, obtained from energy differences between reference and distorted complex gradient maps on strong-edge regions and accumulated over weakly-structured ones, reflecting the perceptual leakage of degraded edges into surrounding textures. Crucially, $μ$ is the only information extracted from the reference and is computed once per image pair, reducing the reference footprint to its information-theoretic minimum. The final score is produced by an affine fusion with only three interpretable parameters, making the method compact, transparent, and computationally efficient, with the viewing distance embedded into the operator scale and no dataset-specific calibration. Extensive evaluations on six standard benchmarks show that PreSPA consistently rivals or exceeds leading No-Reference approaches, while in several cases matching the accuracy of Full-Reference models.
