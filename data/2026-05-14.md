### [1] [Turbulent oscillation in unbalanced T-junction flows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12621)
*Dongjie Jia,Arezoo Ardekani*

Main category: physics.flu-dyn

TL;DR: High-fidelity simulations of unbalanced, high-Reynolds-number T-junction flows reveal a previously unreported oscillatory interaction between the two inlet streams that produces a new turbulence-production mode. The oscillation persists across Re and has an approximately constant Strouhal number, leading many flow metrics to scale as power laws with Re.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing literature focuses on balanced T-junction turbulence or low-to-moderate Reynolds numbers. Practical systems (e.g., T-junction micromixers for nanoparticle synthesis) often operate with large inlet flow-rate imbalance and turbulent Re, leaving a gap in understanding of their fluid dynamics and mixing performance.

Method: High-fidelity numerical simulations (presumably DNS/LES) of unbalanced T-junction geometries across a range of high Re and inlet flow-rate ratios. Time-resolved analysis revealed oscillatory dynamics and measurements of frequency (Strouhal), energy production, and scaling of parameters with Re.

Result: Identification of a new oscillatory mode between the inlet streams that acts as a turbulence-production mechanism. The oscillation persists across Re with nearly constant Strouhal number, and several flow parameters obey Re power-law relations. Evidence contrasts with prior balanced-flow behavior.

Conclusion: The discovery implies revisions to modeling and design of T-junction mixers: turbulent/mixing predictions and process quality depend on this unbalanced-oscillation mechanism. Further validation and parametric studies are needed to generalize results and translate to design guidelines.

Abstract: The T-junction impinging flow occurs in many fluid dynamics systems. In particular, the T-junction micromixer has recently been widely used for nanoparticle production, where the two inlet streams operate at a significant flow-rate imbalance and the Reynolds number is in the turbulent regime. This operating condition exposes a gap in the existing literature on the fluid dynamics of the T-junction. In this study, we used high-fidelity numerical simulations to investigate high-Reynolds-number unbalanced T-junction flows. We discover a new oscillatory behavior between the two inlet streams at the T-junction, leading to a new turbulence-production mode. We will present detailed evidence of this new behavior, in contrast to the existing understanding of balanced turbulent T-junction flows. This oscillatory behavior also persists across a range of Reynolds numbers simulated, where the Strouhal number is approximately constant, indicating a self-similar phenomenon. As a result, many of the fluid dynamics parameters follow a power-law relation with the Reynold number. The discovery in this paper affects real-world applications, where process design and product quality are affected by turbulence and mixing dynamics.
