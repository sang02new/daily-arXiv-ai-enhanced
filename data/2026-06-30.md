TL;DR: Residual false positives in prostate MRI share imaging contrast with true cancers and this property reproduces across five different model backbones and modality perturbations; a lightweight post-hoc refinement head (≈89k parameters) improves case-level specificity in-domain in some folds but shows fold-conditional behavior and no clear gain on an external saturated dataset.

Motivation: To determine whether residual false positives arise from model artifacts or from inherent imaging-level similarity to cancers, and to test whether a small post-hoc refinement head can reduce false positives at the case level without reducing sensitivity.

Method: Retrospective study using PI-CAI (5-fold cross-validation) and external Prostate158 (n=158). Trained a context-aware evidence head and an 89,216-parameter refinement head on a frozen detection backbone; also trained the evidence head on four additional backbones (nnU-Net, U-Net, Mamba variants). Compared contrast ratios (T2, ADC, high-b) of false-positive regions vs ground-truth lesions and contralateral benign regions. Evaluated case-level specificity and sensitivity, including fold-wise cross-validation and McNemar test on external data.

Result: False positives were significantly closer in imaging contrast to true cancers than to benign tissue across five architectures and across modality perturbations (Cohen's d=1.10; FP/benign evidence ratio 2.38×; reproduced 35/35 and 105/105 comparisons). On one PI-CAI fold refinement increased specificity from 0.469 to 0.549 at preserved sensitivity (0.943); cross-validation showed mixed fold-wise effects (9/15 positive, range −22% to +28%). On Prostate158 both models saturated (McNemar pooled p=0.69) though contrast-matching of FPs replicated.

Conclusion: Residual false positives are a data-level imaging phenomenon (contrast-matched to cancer) rather than model-specific artifacts. A lightweight post-hoc refinement head can yield practical specificity gains in-domain but its benefit is fold-conditional and did not generalize when models reached saturation on external data.

Abstract: Objectives: To characterize residual false positives in prostate MRI detection, and to evaluate a lightweight post-hoc refinement head for case-level specificity. Materials and Methods: This retrospective study used PI-CAI (5-fold cross-validation) and Prostate158 (n=158; external). A context-aware evidence head and an 89,216-parameter refinement head were trained on a frozen detection backbone; the evidence head was also trained on four further backbones (bare nnU-Net, bare U-Net, bare Mamba, MIGF-Mamba). For each false-positive region, T2-weighted, apparent-diffusion-coefficient, and high-b-value contrast ratios versus peri-lesional rings were compared against ground-truth lesions and contralateral benign regions. Results: False positives were closer to true cancers than to benign tissue in evidence and raw T2-weighted and apparent-diffusion-coefficient contrast, reproducing 35/35 across five architectures (Cohen's d 1.10; FP/benign evidence ratio 2.38x) and 105/105 across modality-perturbation scenarios. On PI-CAI fold-0, refinement raised case-level specificity from 0.469 to 0.549 (+17.2%) at preserved sensitivity (0.943); 5-fold cross-validation showed fold-conditional behavior (9/15 observations positive; range -22% to +28%). On Prostate158, both models saturated (McNemar pooled p=0.69), while the false-positive contrast-matching finding replicated. Conclusion: Residual false positives are contrast-matched to cancer (sharing raw imaging features rather than histologically confirmed mimicry), reproducing across five architectures -- a data-level imaging property, not model-specific artifacts; post-hoc refinement adds practical specificity in-domain but is fold-conditional.
