### [1] [Self-organised structures in mixed active-passive suspensions due to hydrodynamic interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23555)
*Alexander Chamolly,Takuji Ishikawa*

Main category: physics.flu-dyn

TL;DR: Hydrodynamically-coupled 3D Stokesian dynamics of bottom-heavy squirmers mixed with passive spheres shows passive particles generally disrupt orientational order; phase separation is metastable for neutral/puller squirmers at high density, while bottom-heaviness can drive dynamic phase separation producing fibrillar patterns at medium density and a novel lamellar sandwich structure for strong-bottom-heavy pullers at high density.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Understand how hydrodynamic many-body interactions control self-organisation and phase separation in dense mixed suspensions of active squirmers and passive obstacles, a problem unexplored due to computational difficulty.

Method: Three-dimensional Stokesian dynamics simulations including hydrodynamic interactions of spherical bottom-heavy squirmers and passive obstacle spheres across ranges of packing density and swimmer type (neutral, puller, pusher) and bottom-heaviness; track microstructure and transport.

Result: Passive spheres tend to disturb orientational order unless external orienting torques are present; neutral and puller squirmers can exhibit metastable phase-separated states at high packing; bottom-heavy swimmers can dynamically induce phase separation: fibrillar separation for neutral/pullers at medium densities and a lamellar sandwich-like separation for strongly bottom-heavy pullers at high densities.

Conclusion: Hydrodynamic interactions and swimmer type critically determine microstructure and transport in mixed suspensions; external torques (bottom-heaviness) enable control of phase behaviour and novel separation morphologies.

Abstract: Microswimmers in suspension exhibit collective swimming behaviour, forming various self-organised structures including ordered, aggregated, and turbulent-like structures. When mixed with passive particles phase-separation is known to occur, but due to the difficulty of accurately handling many-body hydrodynamic interactions, the formation of self-organised structures in mixed suspensions has remained unexplored so far. In this study, we investigate the dynamics of mixed dense suspensions of spherical bottom-heavy squirmers and obstacle spheres using Stokesian dynamics in three dimensions, taking hydrodynamic interactions into account. The results show that without an external orientating mechanism the formation of orientational order is in general disturbed by the presence of passive spheres. An initially phase-separated state is metastable for neutral or puller squirmers at high packing densities. When the squirmers are bottom-heavy, phase-separation can occur dynamically in some cases, notably a fibrillar kind of separation for neutral squirmers and pullers at medium densities. We also observed a novel form of lamellar phase-separation for pullers at high densities with strong bottom-heaviness, with a sandwich-like structure consisting of a layer of passive particles pushed by a layer of swimmers, followed by a gap. These results indicate that microstructure and particle transport undergo significant changes depending on the type of swimmer, highlighting the importance of hydrodynamic interactions. These insights allow for a deeper understanding of the behaviour of active particles in complex fluids and to control them using external torques.

The results highlight the controlling role of the top boundary layer in setting the mean stratification and clarify the conditions under which HC suppresses RBC.
