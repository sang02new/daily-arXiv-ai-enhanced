Motivation: Sparse, anisotropic MRI slices are often not aligned to the clinically relevant anatomy, reducing diagnostic accuracy. Resampling onto anatomically-aligned planes should present the full target anatomy in-plane and improve radiological grading.

Method: Reconstruct volumetric representation via 3D Gaussian Splatting from sparse MRIs; render novel imaging planes optimally aligned with target anatomy; use these resampled images to predict ordinal stenosis grades; compare performance to Voxel Interpolation (inverse-distance weighted nearest-neighbour).

Result: Across all stenosis conditions tested, images resampled with Gaussian Splatting produced more accurate stenosis gradings than both the original raw scans and scans resampled using Voxel Interpolation.

Conclusion: Gaussian Splatting-based volumetric reconstruction and anatomic-plane resampling can improve diagnostic grading on sparse spinal MRIs and outperform simple voxel interpolation baselines.

Abstract: The objective of this paper is to improve radiological gradings measured on MRIs of spines, by resampling scans so that the new view planes are better aligned with the target anatomy than the original sparse images. The novel view plane is optimal for diagnostic radiological grading of the target anatomy, whereas the original MRI is not. The resampled scans are then used to predict ordinal severity grades of localised stenosis conditions in spinal MRIs. We compare our method against Voxel Interpolation resampling, which takes the average of inverse-distance weighted nearest neighbour intensities for each target coordinate.
