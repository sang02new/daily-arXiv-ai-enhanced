Main category: physics.med-ph

TL;DR: Female-specific haematocrit within the physiological range (≈40%) produces statistically detectable but very small differences in coronary CFD metrics versus a standard rheology model; haemodynamic impact is negligible so standard rheology is sufficient for most coronary CFD studies.

Motivation: Haematocrit affects blood viscosity and therefore CFD-derived coronary haemodynamics. Prior work used broad or pathological haematocrit ranges; it is unclear whether normal, female-specific haematocrit variation meaningfully alters coronary CFD outputs.

Method: Fifteen female coronary models (healthy and stenosed: mild, moderate, severe) were analysed. A haematocrit-dependent Carreau–Yasuda rheology model was developed. CFD runs compared a standard rheology model with a female-specific haematocrit-based model (40%). Metrics computed: time-averaged endothelial shear stress (TAESS), ESS gradient (ESSG), temporal shear variation index (TSVI), helicity, and low/high TAESS exposure across trees, segments, bifurcations, and stenoses. Statistical tests included paired comparisons, Bland–Altman analysis, and linear regression relating inter-model differences to hemodynamic magnitudes.

Result: The female-specific model yielded statistically significant differences (p<0.05) across all investigated metrics and regions, but absolute differences were small. Bland–Altman analyses showed narrow bias and limits of agreement. Regression showed significant associations between inter-model differences and hemodynamic magnitude for TAESS, ESSG, helicity intensity, and adverse TAESS exposure, but with small slopes. Stenosed arteries showed comparable flow disturbances between models across severities.

Conclusion: Physiological female-specific haematocrit variation is computationally detectable but haemodynamically negligible in coronary CFD. Standard rheology models are likely sufficient for most coronary CFD studies; personalised haematocrit modelling is recommended mainly for patients with abnormal haematocrit or for studies focusing on rheology.

Abstract: Haematocrit influences blood viscosity and may affect coronary computational fluid dynamics (CFD).
