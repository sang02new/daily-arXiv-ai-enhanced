### [1] [Comparative analysis of resistive immersed surface and immersed boundary methods for aortic valve simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01662)
*Han Zhao,Alexander D. Kaiser,Fanwei Kong,Aaron L. Brown,Zinan Hu,David Codoni,Sujal Dave,Alison L. Marsden*

Main category: physics.flu-dyn

TL;DR: Comparative study of Resistive Immersed Surface (RIS) with prescribed kinematics vs fully coupled Immersed Boundary (IB) method for aortic valve CFD/FSI: RIS reproduces large-scale flow and mean transvalvular pressure gradients within 15% (5% when inlet BC matched) while reducing computational cost ~60%.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To evaluate whether a simpler prescribed-kinematics RIS approach can provide accurate hemodynamic predictions for realistic aortic valve geometries, compared to more expensive fully coupled IB FSI, enabling faster simulations when leaflet kinematics are known.

Method: Implemented RIS in SimVascular svMultiPhysics (surface with penalty forcing toward prescribed leaflet velocity) and a coupled IB workflow in IBAMR (elastic immersed leaflets) for trileaflet and bicuspid valve models. Compared bulk flow structures and transvalvular pressure gradients under matched and unmatched inlet conditions, and measured computational cost.

Result: RIS captured large-scale flow structures and predicted mean transvalvular pressure gradients within ~15% relative error to IB simulations; error reduced to ~5% when inlet BCs matched. RIS simulations were ~60% cheaper in computational cost.

Conclusion: RIS is a computationally efficient alternative to fully coupled IB for predicting large-scale hemodynamics and mean pressure gradients when representative leaflet kinematics are available, but it likely does not replace IB when detailed leaflet deformation/stress or two-way FSI effects are essential.

Abstract: Numerical modeling of aortic valve dynamics is essential for understanding the complex fluid-structure interaction (FSI) governing valve biomechanics in health and disease. Immersed methods provide a flexible computational framework for simulating the large deformations of valve leaflets and associated blood flow without requiring body-fitted meshes. Among these approaches, the Resistive Immersed Surface (RIS) and Immersed Boundary (IB) methods are widely used. However, systematic comparative analysis of these methods for realistic aortic valve simulations has not been performed. In this work, we compare a prescribed-kinematics RIS workflow implemented in SimVascular's svMultiPhysics solver with a fully coupled IB workflow using IBAMR for trileaflet and bicuspid aortic valve configurations. The RIS method represents the valve as a surface with prescribed kinematics embedded in the fluid domain and introduces a penalty force that drives the surrounding fluid velocity toward the prescribed leaflet velocity. This formulation reduces modeling complexity and provides useful hemodynamic predictions when representative leaflet kinematics are available. In contrast, the IB method models the leaflets as elastic structures fully immersed in the fluid domain and resolves leaflet deformation through fully coupled two-way FSI. The study focuses on the extent to which RIS reproduces bulk hemodynamic features and transvalvular pressure gradients. Results show that the RIS method captures the large-scale flow structures and predicts the mean transvalvular pressure gradient with a relative error within 15% of the fully coupled IB simulation, improving to within 5% when inlet boundary conditions are matched, while reducing computational cost by approximately 60%.

### [2] [Quantum-Inspired Vision: Leveraging Wave-Particle Duality for Low-Illumination Enhancement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01731)
*Yiquan Gao*

Main category: eess.IV

TL;DR: The paper extends Data Relativistic Uncertainty (DRU) by introducing a physics-to-AI paradigm that models images as probabilistic wave functions and uses wave–particle duality to explain DRU’s handling of illumination uncertainty, claiming improved XAI interpretability and robustness to noise.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: DRU needs a firmer theoretical foundation linking physical photon-level uncertainty to AI image enhancement; formalizing a physics-to-AI mapping can clarify mechanisms and provide explainability.

Method: Replace deterministic image representation with complex-valued probabilistic wavefunctions ψ(x); derive a measurement model tying observed intensities to |ψ|^2 with noise (e.g., Poisson/Gaussian); apply DRU inference (e.g., Bayesian/variational) in this Hilbert-space representation and use wave–particle duality concepts to construct interpretable modules.

Result: The paradigm claims to (1) make DRU’s operation more interpretable via physics-based modules, (2) reduce illumination bias by modeling photon uncertainty explicitly, and (3) maintain robustness to data noise through probabilistic inference.

Conclusion: The idea is promising for XAI and robustness, but needs precise mathematical formulation, clear algorithmic details, and thorough empirical validation against standard baselines and under controlled illumination/noise conditions.

Abstract: This study provides a theoretical expansion of the recent Data Relativistic Uncertainty (DRU) framework by formalizing a physics-to-AI paradigm for image enhancement. By modeling images as probabilistic wave functions rather than deterministic states, the paradigm explicitly integrates wave-particle duality to illustrate the system flow of how DRU leverages the intrinsic physical uncertainty of light, a dimension requiring further theoretical discussion. Consequently, this paradigm provides a rigorous Explainable AI (XAI) approach that enhances the interpretability of how DRU mitigates illumination bias and maintains robustness against data noise.

### [3] [Population-Scale Segmentation of Penile Tissue in DIXON MRI using Deep Learning for Quantitative Phenotyping in Male Reproductive Health](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02127)

Main category: eess.IV

TL;DR: MRI 기반의 전체 음경(외부+내부) 자동 분할을 위해 3D nnU-Net을 학습시켜 UK Biobank 34,412명에 대규모 적용한 연구. 교차검증 Dice=0.90, 독립 테스트 Dice=0.92, Hausdorff=3.58, 장기적 반복성 r=0.87.

  <summary>Details</summary>

MRI enables volumetric assessment of the whole penis in vivo, but automated segmentation has not previously been established at population scale. Automated whole-organ volumetry would enable high-throughput phenotyping for multi-omics and clinical studies of male reproductive disease.
  Here, we present a deep learning framework for whole-penis segmentation in multi-channel DIXON MRI. Using a newly curated expert-annotated training dataset ($n = 145$ subjects; $13,050$ annotated slices) and a double-annotated independent test benchmark ($n = 24$ subjects; $2,160$ double-annotated slices), we optimized a 3D nnU-Net architecture.   We deployed the model in $34,412$ UK Biobank participants, enabling automated quantification of total penile tissue, including both external and internal components. Longitudinal evaluation in 2,282 men demonstrated high inter-session reproducibility ($r = 0.87$). This framework establishes a reproducible and population-scalable method for MRI-based assessment of penile anatomy and provides an open technical resource for future studies in urological imaging and male reproductive health. The trained model weights will be publicly released.

### [4] [Self-Auditing Residual Drifting for Pathology-Preserving Accelerated Knee MRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02428)
*Qing Lyu,Jianxu Wang,Mohammad Kawas,Ge Wang,Christopher T. Whitlow*

Main category: eess.IV

TL;DR: SA-RDM-DC is a self-auditing residual generative drifting model with data consistency for accelerated knee MRI that predicts image- and missing-k-space residuals, enforces physics consistency, outputs dense error maps and slice-level risk scores, and achieves top SSIM while keeping subsecond per-slice inference.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Undersampled MRI reconstruction can blur or hallucinate diagnostically relevant features; global metrics (e.g., PSNR/SSIM) may not capture clinically relevant failures or case-specific risk. A fast reconstruction that preserves pathology and provides per-case reliability signals is needed.

Method: Adapt the generative drifting paradigm to MRI by training a physics-conditioned drift field from zero-filled reconstructions toward fully sampled residual corrections. Predict both image-domain and missing-k-space residuals, use frequency-aware and residual drifting supervision, enforce k-space data consistency, and produce dense error maps plus slice-level risk scores in one inference pass.

Result: On multi-coil fastMRI knee data (AF 4,8,12) and SKM-TEA protocol-shift tests, SA-RDM-DC attains highest SSIM vs multiple baselines, preserves lesion-region structural fidelity, reduces meniscus prediction instability, yields strong self-auditing signals that identify high-error cases, and runs in subsecond per-slice inference (avoiding long diffusion sampling).

Conclusion: SA-RDM-DC offers a balance of image fidelity, pathology preservation, runtime efficiency, and case-specific reliability; recommended to evaluate reconstructions beyond global metrics using pathology-aware and self-auditing criteria.

Abstract: Accelerated magnetic resonance imaging reduces acquisition time, but reconstruction from undersampled k-space can blur diagnostically relevant structures or introduce failures that are not captured by global image metrics. We propose SA-RDM-DC, a Self-Auditing Residual generative Drifting Model with Data Consistency for accelerated knee MRI. The method adapts the newly proposed generative drifting paradigm to accelerated MRI by training a physics-conditioned drift field from the zero-filled reconstruction toward the fully sampled residual correction. It predicts image- and missing-k-space residual corrections, enforces data consistency with acquired k-space, uses frequency-aware and residual drifting supervision to recover fine detail, and produces dense error maps and slice-level risk scores in the same inference pass. We evaluate SA-RDM-DC on multi-coil fastMRI knee data at acceleration factors of 4, 8, and 12, with fastMRI+ pathology annotations for region-level and classifier-based task preservation, and on SKM-TEA for zero-shot and fine-tuned protocol-shift evaluation. Compared with zero-filled reconstruction, UNet-image-SENSE, DC-UNet, Score-Diffusion, ELF-Diff, SENSE-VarNet, and MoDL baselines, SA-RDM-DC achieves the highest SSIM across fastMRI acceleration factors while retaining subsecond per-slice inference and avoiding the long sampling time of iterative diffusion baselines. In pathology-aware analysis, SA-RDM-DC preserves lesion-region structural fidelity and reduces meniscus prediction instability. Its self-auditing scores strongly identify high-error reconstructions on fastMRI and partially transfer as a selective-review signal under SKM-TEA protocol shift. These results support reconstruction evaluation that jointly considers image fidelity, pathology preservation, runtime, and case-specific reliability.
